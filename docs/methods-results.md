## Upstream workflow

- **Study design and count ingestion**
  - **Methods:** We analyzed PipSeq V T2 data from six Mouse × Condition samples—paired mice 10 and 3, mouse 30 in the p27CKO + E-Stim condition only, and mouse 33 in the p27CKO control condition only—against a custom reference containing enhanced green fluorescent protein (eGFP). We read six matched 10X-format directories with `Seurat::Read10X(gene.column = 2, unique.features = TRUE)`, reconciled folder identifiers exactly with the `Sample`, `Mouse`, and `Condition` metadata, and created the count object with `CreateSeuratObject(min.cells = 1, min.features = 1)`.
  - **Results:** The six matrices contained 895,972 barcodes and 38,394 gene features.

- **Cell calling and doublet identification**
  - **Methods:** We calculated mitochondrial and ribosomal percentages, ranked barcodes with `DropletUtils::barcodeRanks()`, and called cells with `DropletUtils::emptyDrops()` at a false-discovery rate (FDR) of 0.01. We required at least 108 counts and 99 features before doublet testing, ran sample-aware `scDblFinder` with an expected doublet rate of 0.01 and seed 1312, and retained singlet calls for subsequent quality control.
  - **Results:** `emptyDrops()` called 5,459 cells; `scDblFinder` classified 5,178 singlets and 210 doublets; 71 called cells lacked a singlet/doublet result.
  <!-- Evidence: Methods=scripts/01-regenerate-frozen.R current-QC stage (percent.mt, percent.ribo, barcodeRanks, emptyDrops, MIN_CELL_COUNTS=108, MIN_CELL_FEATURES=99, scDblFinder samples='Sample', dbr=0.01, seed from config=1312); Results=$MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/qc/sample_qc_attrition.tsv TOTAL row (n_is_cell_true, n_singlet, n_doublet, n_missing_singlet). -->
- **Quality-control filtering**
  - **Methods:** We calculated sample-specific lower thresholds for counts and features as the median minus three median absolute deviations on the log10 scale and calculated the upper mitochondrial threshold as the median plus three median absolute deviations. We retained only called singlets at or above both lower thresholds and at or below the mitochondrial threshold.
  - **Results:** We excluded 1,276 singlets that failed at least one count, feature, or mitochondrial criterion and retained 3,902 cells (sample identifiers and retained counts: S2, 315; S3, 1,225; S4, 463; S5, 352; S7, 536; S8, 1,011). Across the six Mouse × Condition samples, the mean sample-level values were 25,485 unique molecular identifiers, 5,986 detected features, and 4.13% mitochondrial RNA.
  <!-- Evidence: Methods=scripts/01-regenerate-frozen.R QC threshold and pass_qc stage; Results=$MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/qc/sample_qc_attrition.tsv, $MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/qc/post_filtering_qc_stats.tsv, and $MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/for_manuscript/supp_table_sample_qc.xlsx. -->
- **Normalization, variable-feature selection, and principal component analysis**
  - **Methods:** We created four branches from the same 3,902 filtered cells by crossing two normalization methods—Seurat log1p and `scclrR::pflog`—with two cell-cycle-gene treatments: retaining or removing mouse S-, G2-, and M-phase genes from an initial set of 2,000 highly variable genes selected by variance-stabilizing transformation. We calculated 50 principal components for each branch and supplied seed 1312 to PFlog principal component analysis.
  - **Results:** We generated four 3,902-cell branch objects. The unfiltered branches retained 2,000 highly variable genes, and the cell-cycle-filtered source branches retained 1,931.
  <!-- Evidence: Methods=scripts/01-regenerate-frozen.R source-preprocessing stage; R/seurat-methods.R::run_log1p_pca() and run_pflog_pca(); Results=$MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/frozen_object_numbers.tsv. -->
- **Source clustering parameter sweep**
  - **Methods:** We built shared-nearest-neighbor graphs from 20, 30, or 50 principal components and ran Leiden clustering through Seurat/igraph at resolutions 0.3, 0.5, and 0.8 for all four preprocessing branches. We calculated uniform manifold approximation and projection embeddings and descriptive summaries of cluster-tree and grid stability. For source clustering, we used PFlog with cell-cycle genes retained, 20 principal components, and resolution 0.3.
  - **Results:** The sweep produced 36 candidate clusterings; the selected source configuration contained 3,902 cells in eight clusters.
  <!-- Evidence: Methods=scripts/01-regenerate-frozen.R source-clustering stage; Results=$MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/cluster/cluster_grid_summary.tsv and $MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/tables/frozen_object_numbers.tsv; R/config.R selected source column. -->
- **Marker- and Cdkn1b-based cluster exclusion**
  - **Methods:** We calculated curated retinal marker-module scores with `Seurat::AddModuleScore()` and excluded a cluster by marker score only when microglia or photoreceptor ranked first with a score of at least 0.5 and a margin of at least 0.25 over the second score. We tested cluster-level `Cdkn1b` PFlog expression and count detection against all other cells with one-sided Wilcoxon tests and Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment, calling high `Cdkn1b` only when both q values were below 0.05 and detection was at least 20%.
  - **Results:** Cluster 2 met the microglia and high-`Cdkn1b` rules; clusters 7 and 8 met the high-`Cdkn1b` rule; no cluster met the photoreceptor rule. The exclusions removed 548 cells from cluster 2, 79 from cluster 7, and 37 from cluster 8, leaving 3,238 cells in the MG-selected branch.

- **MG-selected reclustering and cell-cycle sensitivity**
  - **Methods:** We reselected 2,000 highly variable genes in the 3,238 retained cells, reran PFlog principal component analysis with 50 principal components, and repeated the Leiden grid across 20, 30, or 50 principal components and resolutions 0.3, 0.5, or 0.8, both with and without cell-cycle genes. For downstream analyses, we used seed 2847 and the clustering that retained cell-cycle genes, used 20 principal components, and set the resolution to 0.3. We used the matching branch without cell-cycle genes as a sensitivity analysis.
  - **Results:** The selected branch had five clusters with 2,201, 486, 238, 208, and 105 cells. All five clusters included both conditions, and four included all mice. The cell-cycle-filtered branch retained 1,925 highly variable genes and produced six clusters at 20 principal components and resolution 0.3.

## Descriptive annotation and cluster composition

- **Curated marker-module and p27 summaries**
  - **Methods:** We averaged curated retinal marker-module scores within each selected cluster and calculated cluster mean p27 expression from the PFlog layer. We standardized p27 means against 2,000 within-Mouse × Condition cluster-label permutations. For descriptive visualization, we displayed per-cell marker expression as row z scores and in feature plots based on uniform manifold approximation and projection.
  - **Results:** Müller glia module scores were positive in clusters 1, 2, 4, and 5. Cluster 2 had the highest neurogenic-progenitor score, cluster 3 had the highest cone-bipolar score, and cluster 5 had the highest activated-Müller-glia and proliferative scores. Standardized p27 enrichment scores (z scores) ranged from −3.04 in cluster 4 to 3.20 in cluster 1.

- **Cluster marker analysis**
  - **Methods:** We treated each of the five selected numeric clusters as a separate, unmerged identity and used `Seurat::FindAllMarkers()` to run Wilcoxon tests for positive markers only on the RNA data layer, with `min.pct = 0.01`, `logfc.threshold = 0.1`, and a Bonferroni-adjusted p-value cutoff of 0.01. Within each cluster, we ranked markers by absolute average log2 fold change, adjusted p value, raw p value, and gene name, then retained five markers for the dotplot.
  - **Results:** Clusters 1–5 contained 3,040, 750, 3,311, 661, and 2,657 significant positive markers, respectively. We did not assign an interpreted identity to any cluster from this test.

- **Pooled cluster abundance (descriptive)**
  - **Methods:** We pooled cells by condition, calculated centered-log-ratio log2 enrichment with a 0.5 pseudocount, and tested pooled cluster-by-condition counts with Fisher exact tests. We adjusted across clusters by Holm and counted cells, rather than Mouse × Condition samples, as the units.
  - **Results:** Pooled counts showed clusters 1 and 2 enriched under E-Stim (log2 enrichment, 0.867 and 1.083; Holm-adjusted p values, 2.16 × 10^-5 and 0.00451), clusters 4 and 5 depleted (−0.470 and −1.916; adjusted p values, 1.69 × 10^-8 and 4.66 × 10^-15), and no significant pooled difference for cluster 3 (adjusted p = 0.138).

- **Mouse × Condition cluster proportions**
  - **Methods:** We calculated each cluster’s proportion within each Mouse × Condition sample, connected paired mice 10 and 3 in the descriptive plot, tested paired differences on stabilized logits with two-sided exact sign randomization, and adjusted across five clusters by Benjamini–Hochberg. We ran a fixed singleton-inclusive sensitivity with the same two-sided procedure using mouse 33 as control-only and mouse 30 as E-Stim-only.
  - **Results:** Paired mean proportions increased for clusters 1 and 2 and decreased for clusters 3–5, but no cluster reached q < 0.05; paired q values were 0.625 for clusters 1, 3, 4, and 5 and 1.0 for cluster 2. The singleton-inclusive sensitivity also produced no q value below 0.625.

## Condition-level expression and enrichment

- **Primary pseudobulk differential expression**
  - **Methods:** We aggregated RNA counts for each Mouse × Condition pseudobulk sample, retained genes with at least 10 counts in at least three of the six pseudobulk samples, fitted DESeq2 with `~ condition`, tested E-Stim versus p27CKO, and shrank log2 fold changes with `apeglm`. We called genes significant at a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery rate below 0.05 without a fold-change cutoff; positive log2 fold change denoted higher expression with E-Stim.
  - **Results:** The model tested 17,043 genes and identified 448 significant genes, including 139 higher and 309 lower under E-Stim; eight curated marker-list genes were significant. Increased genes were `Glul` (shrunken log2 fold change 0.936, adjusted p = 1.10 × 10^-5), `Ccn1` (0.718, 0.00490), `Hes6` (0.678, 0.0174), and `Grm6` (1.77, 0.0106). Decreased genes were `Mcm2` (−1.83, 8.30 × 10^-6), `Mcm6` (−0.0543, 0.0267), `Pcna` (−0.914, 0.00259), and `Rcvrn` (−2.10, 0.00264).
  <!-- Evidence: Methods=scripts/04-de-enrichment.R pseudobulk and primary-DE stages; Results=$MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/degs/mg_selected/pseudobulk_sample_table.tsv, numbers.json, deseq2_full_results.tsv, and deseq2_marker_overlap.tsv. -->
- **Paired pseudobulk sensitivity analysis**
  - **Methods:** We restricted the analysis to mice 10 and 3, used four Mouse × Condition pseudobulk samples, refiltered genes at a total count of at least 10, and fitted DESeq2 with `~ mouse + condition`. We tested E-Stim versus p27CKO, shrank log2 fold changes with `apeglm`, and called genes significant at a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate threshold below 0.05; positive log2 fold change denoted higher expression with E-Stim.
  - **Results:** The paired model identified 125 significant genes, including 27 with higher expression and 98 with lower expression under E-Stim. In this model, `Glul` (shrunken log2 fold change 1.04, adjusted p = 0.00387) and `Ccn1` (0.815, 0.0282) had significantly higher expression, whereas `Mcm2` (−1.29, 0.00188) and `Mcm6` (−1.61, 3.97 × 10^-4) had significantly lower expression. The reported adjusted p values use the Benjamini–Hochberg method. `Hes6`, `Pcna`, `Grm6`, and `Rcvrn` were not significant in the paired model.
  <!-- Evidence: Methods=scripts/04-de-enrichment.R paired-sensitivity stage; Results=$MEGAN_SC_DATA_DIR/degs/mg_selected/design_summary.tsv, numbers.json, deseq2_paired_sensitivity_significant_degs.tsv, and deseq2_paired_sensitivity_marker_overlap.tsv. -->
- **Gene Ontology Biological Process over-representation**
  - **Methods:** We split genes with Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted p < 0.05 in the primary Mouse × Condition pseudobulk differential-expression model into genes with higher or lower expression under E-Stim according to shrunken log2 fold-change direction. We mapped mouse gene symbols to Entrez identifiers and used all mapped genes tested in that model as the universe. We ran `clusterProfiler::enrichGO()` for Gene Ontology Biological Process with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment, retained terms with adjusted p < 0.05, and reduced redundant terms with `clusterProfiler::simplify()`.
  - **Results:** The simplified set for genes with higher expression under E-Stim contained one term, angiogenesis (15 of 121 mapped genes; adjusted p = 0.0184). The simplified set for genes with lower expression under E-Stim contained 66 terms led by chromosome segregation, nuclear division, nuclear chromosome segregation, and DNA replication.

- **Preranked Gene Ontology Biological Process enrichment**
  - **Methods:** We ranked all mapped genes tested in the primary Mouse × Condition pseudobulk differential-expression model by the DESeq2 Wald statistic for E-Stim versus p27CKO, with positive statistics denoting higher expression under E-Stim. For each Entrez identifier, we retained the gene symbol associated with the largest absolute statistic. We ran `clusterProfiler::gseGO()` with gene-set sizes of 10–500, seed 1312, and Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment, retained terms with adjusted p < 0.05, and then simplified the retained terms.
  - **Results:** The simplified table contained 337 significant terms, including 268 with positive normalized enrichment scores and 69 with negative scores. Positive terms, enriched toward genes with higher expression under E-Stim, included small-molecule catabolism, lipid catabolism, and energy derivation by oxidation of organic compounds; the strongest negative terms, enriched toward genes with lower expression under E-Stim, included chromosome segregation and nuclear division.

- **Bayesian term selection (exploratory)**

  - **Results:** The Bayesian analysis classified no term from the higher-expression gene table as active and classified one term from the lower-expression gene table, CENP-A-containing chromatin assembly, as active.

## Noncanonical sensitivity analysis

- **Exploratory targeted neurogenic-proportion analysis**
  - **Methods:** We used six Mouse × Condition samples from the p27CKO and p27CKO + E-Stim conditions. We defined six gates by crossing neurogenic-progenitor score thresholds above the 80th, 90th, or 95th percentile of the equal-sample-weighted control distribution with proliferation score thresholds below the 25th or 50th percentile of that distribution. For each gate, we fitted a condition-only beta-binomial logit model, compared it with an intercept-only model by likelihood ratio, requested 1,000 parametric-bootstrap simulations, retried failed Hessian checks with the Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno (BFGS) algorithm, and calculated bootstrap p values from successful fits. We adjusted the six p values by Benjamini–Hochberg.
  - **Results:** Every gate had a higher mean target-cell proportion under E-Stim than under control. E-Stim/control mean-proportion ratios ranged from 1.82 to 5.22. Across the six gates, 933–1,000 bootstrap fits per gate succeeded. All Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted bootstrap p values were 0.14.
